The low-maintenance garden isn’t boring

You can have a garden with colour, texture and character without signing up for a new full-time hobby. It starts with the right layout and plants.

Brisbane Landscaping Group · 4 MIN READ · Practical garden advice

EDITORIAL INSPIRATION PHOTO, NOT OUR PROJECT

“Low-maintenance” can sound a bit like three shrubs in gravel. It doesn't have to. A thoughtful Brisbane garden can be lush, colourful and welcoming while still being realistic about time, water and upkeep.

Give your beds a clear shape

Strong edges and well-sized garden beds make a surprising difference. One generous planting area is often easier to look after than lots of little pockets of soil around the lawn. A defined edge also makes mowing and tidying easier.

Choose plants for their spot

A shady fence line and a hot north-facing strip are not the same planting conditions. Before choosing plants, look at how much sun reaches the area, whether water sits there after rain, and how wide each plant will become. Don't be afraid of Australian natives. Brisbane City Council highlights flax lilies, grevilleas, native violets, coastal rosemary and various lilly pillies as options for different garden sizes and situations.

A good mix might use one feature plant, a couple of repeating shrubs and low-growing plants to soften the edges. Repeating a few varieties usually looks more intentional than buying one of everything.

Mulch does more than make it look finished

Fresh mulch creates an instant clean look, but it's useful too: it helps hold moisture, reduces soil temperature and can suppress weeds. Keep it away from trunks and stems and check that drainage is not blocked. Organic mulch slowly breaks down and benefits the soil.

Keep the plan flexible: If the budget is tight, do the edging and soil preparation now, then add plants in stages. A neat, well-prepared bed is a good foundation.

A garden should fit your everyday life

Think about dogs, children, leaf litter around pools, access to taps and whether you actually want a lawn to mow. A stepping-stone path or a mulched area can sometimes solve a daily annoyance better than more planting.
