A happier lawn through a Brisbane summer

A lush lawn is a lovely thing. Keeping it healthy through Brisbane heat, rain and the occasional dry spell takes a little know-how — not endless weekend work.

Brisbane Landscaping Group · 4 MIN READ · Practical garden advice

EDITORIAL INSPIRATION PHOTO, NOT OUR PROJECT

Most Brisbane lawns live through a bit of everything: hot afternoons, summer downpours, shady corners and long stretches when nobody remembers the hose. The secret is not to throw every lawn product at the problem. Start by working out what your grass needs and what the site is actually like.

1. Start with the grass, not the fertiliser

If you're putting in a new lawn, think about sunlight first. Buffalo and zoysia are often worth considering where there is some shade, while couch usually wants more direct sun. Every yard is different, though — trees, pets, slope and foot traffic matter just as much as the grass name on the label.

For thin, tired patches, don't automatically replace the whole lawn. Check whether the trouble is compacted ground, too much shade, drainage or simple wear. Sometimes improving the soil and repairing sections is the more sensible answer.

2. Water less often, but properly

A quick sprinkle on top might look reassuring, but it often does little for the roots. Queensland's waterwise advice recommends watering when the lawn needs it and soaking more deeply rather than giving it tiny daily drinks. Early morning is a sensible choice when watering is permitted; always check current local restrictions.

3. Don't mow it into the ground

Very short cuts can leave the soil exposed to the harsh sun. Keep mower blades sharp and adjust the cutting height to suit your turf. Buffalo lawns generally prefer a taller cut than couch. In the heat of the day, the mower can wait.

Our simple rule: Look at drainage, sunlight and soil before spending money on fresh turf. A lawn that suits the spot is easier to keep green.

4. Plan for the first few weeks

New turf and seeded areas need a different watering routine while they establish. Your turf supplier's instructions, weather, season and any watering restrictions all matter.
